The News

WASHINGTON, October 19 - Katie Zacharia has departed as White House press secretary after less than a week. The Associated Press reported her exit on October 16, following public doubts from President Donald Trump about whether she should remain in the position. Her departure leaves another transition at the administration’s principal briefing-room podium. [1]

Trump announced her appointment on October 9. She was a communications adviser for Trump Media & Technology Group and had briefly worked as a Department of Homeland Security spokesperson. She succeeded Karoline Leavitt, who left in August. [2]

Zacharia’s October 13 debut illustrated competing expectations of the job. The White House praised her presentation of the president’s agenda. An Anadolu report distributed through Reuters Connect described a briefing of roughly 15 minutes with questions from three outlets. That material is an Anadolu dispatch; distribution through Reuters Connect does not mean Reuters independently verified it. [3] [4]

Axios reported that Trump had previously wanted Maria Bartiromo for the position, but she signaled she would not take it. Its October 17 newsletter also reported that Scott Jennings had been considered before Zacharia’s appointment without receiving an offer. These accounts concern the earlier selection process; neither establishes a new offer after Zacharia’s departure. [5] [6]

Status at this edition’s research cutoff, October 19: no newly appointed successor was verified in the reporting reviewed. Names below are possibilities to examine, not a confirmed White House shortlist. Future announcements may supersede this assessment.

Preliminary Candidate Assessment

The following comparisons are editorial analysis. They evaluate possible fit for the work rather than predict the president’s decision. No interviews, private deliberations or willingness to accept the position are assumed.

Anna Kelly: the continuity option

Kelly is identified as the principal deputy press secretary in biographical reference material. The Guardian names Kelly and Steven Cheung as possible successors. [9] [7]

Analysis: an internal promotion could reduce the time needed to learn press-office routines and coordinate responses. That makes Kelly a useful continuity benchmark. However, proximity to the office does not establish presidential preference or prove readiness for prolonged questioning as its principal public representative. Evidence to watch would include expanded briefing duties and an explicit statement from the administration.

Scott Jennings: the television communicator

Georgetown’s biography identifies Jennings as a CNN political contributor with campaign experience and prior service in the George W. Bush White House. Axios reported prior consideration, not a current appointment or offer. [8] [6]

Analysis: television debate experience may help a spokesperson respond quickly and communicate a consistent argument. The harder test is a change of function: a commentator advances interpretations, while a press secretary must obtain authoritative information, distinguish established policy from speculation and correct mistakes. His willingness to leave existing work and his access to decision-makers would matter more than viral debate clips.

Steven Cheung: communications leadership or podium work?

An official presidential record identifies Cheung as White House communications director. His inclusion here reflects public discussion of possible successors, not evidence that he has agreed to change roles. [10] [7]

Analysis: a senior communications official could offer close coordination between presidential messaging and daily responses. Yet moving an existing manager to the podium can create another vacancy or concentrate two demanding jobs in one person. Remaining communications director while another person handles briefings is a separate, plausible organizational choice. No such arrangement is confirmed here.

Maria Bartiromo: prior interest with an availability obstacle

The reported earlier approach to Bartiromo is a different kind of evidence from a speculative list. Axios said she indicated she would not accept; its newsletter described continuing Fox compensation as a practical obstacle, citing an administration source. [5] [6]

Analysis: past presidential interest makes Bartiromo relevant to understanding the selection criteria, but the reported unwillingness is a substantial qualification. She should be treated as a previously approached figure rather than an active candidate unless fresh reporting shows renewed discussions and a change in availability. Recognition on television cannot, by itself, resolve those issues.

Other Names and the Limits of Market Speculation

Prediction-market coverage also mentioned Hogan Gidley, Katie Miller, Harrison Fields, Danielle Alvarez and Monica Crowley. This is not an official shortlist. [11]

Analysis: a name appearing in a betting contract is weak evidence of an actual personnel discussion. Market prices can change with publicity, thin trading and ambiguous settlement rules. This edition therefore assigns no numerical probabilities and does not convert market visibility into a ranking of presidential preference. More names are not necessarily more information.

What the Next Selection Would Need to Resolve

  • Operational readiness: can the appointee prepare accurate answers across agencies, follow up unanswered questions and maintain a workable briefing schedule?
  • Credibility: are qualifications described accurately, and can errors be corrected promptly with evidence?
  • Presidential access: can the spokesperson reliably establish what the president has decided and distinguish that from personal commentary?
  • Role clarity: will the office mainly promote messages, or also provide sustained, substantive questioning and explanation?
  • Availability: is there a confirmed willingness to serve, with contractual and practical arrangements settled?

Preliminary Judgment

An experienced internal deputy is the clearest continuity model; an established television communicator offers a different presentation model. Neither profile guarantees success. The decisive evidence would be a verified appointment, a clear mandate and an early record of answering difficult factual questions. On the available material, selecting a single favorite would imply more certainty than the evidence supports.

For the next update, watch for an official announcement, an on-record acceptance, assigned acting briefing responsibilities and a published briefing schedule. A reported meeting or expression of interest would narrow the uncertainty, but would still fall short of an appointment.
